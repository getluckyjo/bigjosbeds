# 01 — Brand foundations

## Position
Premium beds for big and tall people who want a bed that fits. The primary audience in the brief is big and tall men and their partners; the public tagline welcomes big people without turning size into a problem.

**Promise:** room to rest, with considered proportions and a supportive product story.
**Brand name:** Big Jo’s Beds. **Primary line:** Best sleep for big people.
**Supporting line:** Room to rest. **Product headline:** Finally, a bed that fits.

## Personality and voice
Confident, warm, direct and lightly humorous. Speak to a person, not a demographic label. Explain dimensions plainly. Premium comes from restraint, proportion, materials and care; it should never sound aloof.

| Use | Avoid |
|---|---|
| “Built for big.” | Medical language or jokes about bodies |
| “More room to stretch out.” | Shame, shrinking, fixing the customer |
| “Choose your finish.” | Technical jargon for simple choices |
| “Made to order in Cape Town.” (brief-supplied) | Unsupported “best in South Africa” claims |
| Specific verified specifications | “Indestructible”, “orthopaedic”, “guaranteed pain relief” |

Never use “bariatric”, “obese” or “plus-size” in brand marketing. Never invent exclusivity or superiority claims from the competitor notes.

## Logo system
The vector lockups preserve the approved board’s large serif wordmark and BEDS descriptor tucked under Jo’s. Use the two-colour `big-jos-primary.svg` on Bone/Paper, the Bone lockup on Clay/Charcoal, and the Ink version for single-colour uses. The favicon is a utility initial, not a new primary brand symbol.

Clear space: at least one capital-B stem width on every side, roughly 8% of the lockup width. Do not box the wordmark except when explicitly placing it on a contrasting solid field. Never add outlines, shadows, stretching or additional taglines into the logo file. Keep the tagline as accessible HTML text.

Minimum lockup width: 150px digital (desktop recommendation 190px); below 150px use the utility favicon only where a full accessible name exists. Keep logo labels legible when used on product cards; do not artificially sharpen the raster product label. The vector paths are reconstructed from a raster board and retain small edge irregularities; check a production proof before embroidery/print.

## Art direction
Warm morning light, plaster, natural wood, tactile weave, restrained Cape Town cues. Show scale through the product, not stereotyped bodies. Photograph the complete mattress and base; use clean detail shots for weave, piping and labels. Product selection images should share angle, crop, scale and light.

Flax: beige woven border and base, clay triple ticking stripes, bone piping and top. Charcoal: warm dark grey woven border and base, tonal graphite ticking, same cream top/piping. The brief prefers a flat knitted top; render texture is directional and must be reconciled with the final sample.

The headboard and props in renders are styling, not confirmed inclusions. The product purchase description must state the included components explicitly.

## Motifs
Triple ticking stripes are the secondary graphic. Use as a 24–32px separator, small packaging edge or restrained background swatch. Do not stretch stripes across text, replace whitespace with texture, or mix multiple decorative motifs. Use a single 1.5–2px stroke icon family when icons are needed; label actions with text.
