# SA heavy-duty, extra-length, bariatric and custom-size beds and bedding (competitor map for Big Jo's Beds)

All prices are ZAR incl. VAT as displayed on the cited page, observed 6 Oct 2026 unless stated otherwise. "Observed Oct 2026" means the page was retrieved on 2026-10-06. Retailer sale prices change often. Treat every price as a snapshot. Where a page contradicts itself or another source, both figures are given.

Reference point for comparison: Big Jo's set is 160 × 210 cm, rated 250 kg per person, with a 180 mm heavy-duty core, 50 mm UHD foam, 20 mm latex, a 100% cotton cover and a heavy-duty base. Supplier cost is about R7,930 incl. VAT.

## 1. Which SA manufacturers/retailers sell mattresses explicitly rated for high weights or marketed as "heavy duty"?

### Takeaway
The "heavy sleeper" niche in SA is real and contested. At least 10 brands or retailers publish per-person weight ratings from 120 kg to 220 kg. Online seller Mr Mattress is pushing hard on "200 kg per sleeper" SEO content and publishes full prices. No competitor found publishes a 250 kg per-person rating. The highest is BlueCross 150 Ultra at "220 kg per sleeping partner", and that figure conflicts with its own retailer's 150 kg spec. Prices for 150–200 kg-rated mattresses run roughly R9k–R13.5k for a queen/king mattress, and about R13k–R15k for a 200 cm-long queen/king set with base.

### Inferences
- Weight ratings in SA are unregulated marketing claims. Several sellers contradict themselves (PBS 100 vs 200 kg; BlueCross 150 vs 220 kg; Bedcorp 140 vs 165–185 kg), and budget sellers claim 200 kg at R2,400–R4,000. Big Jo's "250 kg per person" will be the highest published figure found, but buyers have reason to be sceptical. A third-party test or published spec sheet would set it apart, as Mr Mattress does with SABS density and EN 1957 claims.
- The credible premium heavy-duty set is about R13k–R15k for a 200 cm-long Queen/King with base: Mr Mattress Ultra 200 Queen XL set R13,199 and King XL set R14,699, and Techra mattress-only R12k–R14.5k. Against Big Jo's R7,930 supplier cost, a retail price of about R14k–R18k for a 160×210 set would be roughly 1.8–2.3× cost. That price would need the extra width and length, the 250 kg rating, latex and a cotton cover to justify a premium over Mr Mattress.
- Mr Mattress is the most dangerous competitor for online and SEO demand. It owns "mattress for heavy person South Africa" content, publishes comparison pages naming rivals, offers a 365-night trial and delivers free nationwide.
- No competitor found uses "big man" or "tall man" branding for beds specifically. The nearest is BlueCross/Brothers' "Built for bigger" and Viking's "larger-than-life South Africans". The positioning gap is open.

### Gaps
- No PBS product literally called "Max Weight" was found, and PBS heavy-duty pricing (Instagram) could not be read. Confirm by phone or in-store visit at Montague Gardens.
- No official Sealy, Edblo or Restonic manufacturer spec pages with weight ratings above 150 kg were found. The Forty Winks MegaSleep weight rating was not verified.
- Harris Hotel Heavy Duty pricing is quote-only and was not obtained. Fit for Bed store locations were not captured.
- Viking Beds' factory location and Giant pricing were not verified.
- No competitor was found publishing a 250 kg per-person rating. This rests on searches only and is not exhaustive.

## 2. Who makes extra-length (200 cm, 210 cm+) and custom-size mattresses and bases in SA, especially Cape Town? Prices, lead times, custom premium?

### Takeaway
"Extra length" in SA almost always means 200 cm (Single XL 91×200 to King XL 183×200). Standard SA widths are 91/107/137/152/183 cm, so 160 cm wide is non-standard. Several Cape Town factories (PBS Montague Gardens, Sleepy Time Parow, Beds From Home, Dream Beds and Home) build extra-length or custom pieces in a few days to three weeks. Almost none publishes custom pricing; it is "send measurements for a quote". The published premium for 200 cm over 188 cm is about +10–20%.

### Inferences

## 3. Medical/bariatric mattress suppliers in SA: prices and claims; competitor or different market?

### Takeaway
Bariatric products in SA are sold by medical and mobility suppliers (Arjo, Medop, Mr Mobility, Primacare, BioClin). They are pressure-care mattresses and electric Hi-Lo beds for patients. They are narrow (about 88–107 cm platforms), clinical-looking, and priced around R9k–R12k for a pressure mattress alone. Beds are often "login for price" or quote-only. This is a different market (care and medical-aid driven), not a direct competitor for a premium bedroom set for big men.

### Inferences
- Bariatric medical beds solve a different job: patient handling and pressure care, often for immobile people, frequently funded by medical aid or hire. Their platform widths (around 88 cm for Medop) make them unsuitable as a couple's bedroom bed. These buyers are not Big Jo's core customers, though they show that consumer-grade 200 kg+ products have a ceiling in the R9k–R12k range.
- Mr Mattress straddles both markets: consumer 200 kg and institutional/hospital supply. That makes it the most versatile competitor.

## 6. Rough sizing: share of SA adult men who are tall (>1.9 m) or heavy (obesity, >120 kg)

### Takeaway
SA men are short on average: mean adult height is about 166.7 cm (1996 birth cohort, NCD-RisC via OWID). Men taller than 1.9 m are therefore a very small fraction, likely well under 1%; no direct SA statistic was found. Heavy men are a bigger pool. About 12–15% of SA men are obese (BMI ≥30), and about 4% (around 770,000 men in 2017) have BMI ≥35. Western Cape men have a higher overweight/obesity share (44%) than the national figure (31%).

### Cited Findings
- SADHS 2016: men aged 15+ who are overweight or obese are 31% nationally, 44% in the Western Cape and 26% in the Eastern Cape. — [SADHS 2016 Key Findings (SR248)](https://dhsprogram.com/pubs/pdf/SR248/SR248.pdf)
- Goedecke et al., "Epidemiology of adult obesity" (Clinical Practice Guideline for the Management of Obesity in Adults in South Africa, SAMJ 2025): male obesity (BMI ≥30) was 12.1% in 2017, up from 9.8% in 1998, and 15.3% in 2021 (SA National Food and Nutrition Security Survey). Male overweight (BMI 25–29.9) has been stable at about 22%. In 2017, by class: Class 1 (30–34.9) 7.9%, Class 2 (35–39.9) 2.8%, Class 3 (≥40) 1.1% (the text elsewhere says 8.0/2.9/1.2%). About "770 000 men living with severe obesity [BMI ≥35] in 2017". — [SAMJ 2025](https://samajournals.co.za/index.php/samj/article/download/3616/1555/23484)
- Mean height of SA men: 166.68 cm for the 1996 birth cohort (measured at about age 18 in 2014), up from 165.26 cm for 1896. Source: NCD-RisC via Our World in Data. — [OWID average height of men, ZAF](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/average-height-of-men?tab=table&country=~ZAF)

### Inferences
- **Tall:** 190 cm is about 23 cm above the SA male mean. If height spread is about 7 cm (a typical within-population figure; not sourced here for SA), 190 cm is more than 3 standard deviations above the mean. That suggests well under 1% of SA men, perhaps on the order of 0.1–0.5%, with higher shares likely in some sub-groups. This is a rough estimate, not a measured statistic.
- **Heavy:** BMI ≥35 at about 4% of men (about 770k) is the best available proxy for the ">120 kg" segment. For a 1.85 m man, 120 kg is about BMI 35. The heavy-man segment is therefore much larger than the very-tall segment. The core addressable customer is likely "big" (heavy, broad) more than "very tall", though 210 cm length still matters for men of about 1.85 m and up.
- The Western Cape's 44% overweight/obese share suggests a Cape Town launch is not disadvantaged on this demand driver.

### Gaps
- No SA survey statistic was found for the share of men taller than 1.9 m or for the height standard deviation. A SANHANES or NIDS microdata pull would be needed.
- No SA statistic was found for the share of men weighing more than 120 kg (only BMI classes). The total SA adult male population was not sourced here, so absolute counts beyond the "770,000 with BMI ≥35" figure are not given.
- Income or affordability overlap (heavy or tall men who can afford a R14k+ set) was not researched.
