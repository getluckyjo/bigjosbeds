## Mattress specs (Gary, 6 Oct 08:51)

From the bottom up:

| Layer | Thickness | Spec | What we still need to know |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Heavy-duty core | 180 mm | "Heavy duty core" | Is it foam or pocket springs? Foam needs a density (kg/m³). Springs need a gauge and count. This layer does most of the work at 250 kg. |
| Ultra-high-density foam | 50 mm | "Ultra high density" | The actual density in kg/m³. "Ultra high density" is a label, not a number. |
| Latex | 20 mm | 65 density (kg/m³) | Natural, synthetic or blended? |
| Cover | — | 100% cotton | Is it zip-off? Is the top quilted or flat? What side colours are there? Can it be washed? 100% cotton shrinks. |

That's about 25 cm of foam and core before the cover and quilting. Measure the first unit before briefing anyone on sheets.

**What the specs mean:**
- **The latex is thin.** At 2 cm it's a feel layer on top. Support comes from the 18 cm core and the 5 cm foam, which is consistent with the firm feel you chose.
- **Why latex, not memory foam** (asked at 09:00; compare with Gary's answer):
  - Latex springs back quicker and holds its shape longer under heavy weight. Heavy-duty mattresses most often fail by sagging and leaving body impressions.
  - Latex sleeps cooler. Memory foam softens with body heat and traps it, and big guys tend to run hot.
  - Latex doesn't give the "stuck in the bed" feeling, so it's easier to turn over and get up.
  - The trade-off is less of the slow "hug" that memory-foam fans like.
- **Watch your marketing claims.** With a 2 cm layer, "latex" is a feature, not the whole story. Natural versus synthetic latex also affects which claims you can make.
- **The base is still unspecified.** You asked for base materials at 08:26, and the 08:51 specs only cover the mattress.

## Feasibility notes

**Cash flow.** At the current single-unit price, 10–20 sets a month means paying PBS R79k–R159k upfront every month. To avoid funding stock yourself, take full payment or a deposit from customers before ordering. The 3–5 day lead time makes made-to-order workable.

**Margin per set.** Cost is R7,930, assuming Big Jo's isn't VAT-registered. The prices below are illustrative, not market research, and the margins are before delivery, marketing, payment fees and a warranty reserve.

| Retail price | Gross margin | % | Per month at 10 / 20 sets |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| R12,999 | R5,069 | 39% | R50.7k / R101.4k |
| R14,999 | R7,069 | 47% | R70.7k / R141.4k |
| R16,999 | R9,069 | 53% | R90.7k / R181.4k |

**Size and sheets.** 160 × 210 isn't a standard South African size. Standard queen is 152 × 188, so customers can't buy fitted sheets or protectors off the shelf. Sheets and a protector sold with the bed turn that friction into an add-on. When briefing a sheet maker, give them 160 × 210 and the measured mattress depth (about 25 cm plus the cover), and allow extra pocket depth for a protector.

**Delivery.** A one-piece 160 × 210 base is hard to get into flats and up stairs, which is another reason to ask about a split base.

**Before paying the first invoice.**
- Get the agreed spec and the option you chose in writing on the invoice.
- Phone Gary to confirm the banking details (and branch code) before the first EFT.
- Ask for the invoice to show Big Jo's Beds and the delivery address.
