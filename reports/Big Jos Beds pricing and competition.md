# Big Jo's Beds Can Command Sloom-Level Prices

Big Jo's Beds should sell its 160 × 210 cm heavy-duty set at **R24,999** (mattress alone **R19,999**, base alone R5,999), as one honest price with no fake "was" price. At today's R7,930 supplier cost, that leaves **R17,069 gross profit per set (68.3%)**, or **R16,619 (66.5%)** after PBS's R450 delivery. At that price Big Jo's sits above every South African heavy-duty specialist (Mr Mattress's 200 kg King XL set sells for R14,699) and above the roughly R21k ceiling of mass chains like Sleepmasters. It sits level with the Cape Town DTC leader Sloom (King XL mattress plus frame ≈R28,400) and with Sealy and Simmons firm king sets at Dial-a-Bed (R19.5k–R31k), and well below the R55k+ flagship tier, where brand prestige and technology do the selling. The gap in the market is real. No SA retailer or online brand sells a bed longer than 200 cm, the highest consistently published SA rating is 200 kg per person (one retailer's Facebook posts claim 220 kg for a bed its own product page rates at 150 kg), and even the US heavy-duty leaders stop at California King length. The materials cannot carry a premium on their own, though. Mr Mattress builds a R9,199 queen on a chip-foam core too, and at 25 cm Big Jo's is thinner than every plus-size reference bed. So the price has to be earned with fit, a documented and independently tested 250 kg rating, service the chains don't offer, and a confident "big and tall men" brand. The main risks are that a customer can probably buy something close to this spec from the same factory at roughly Big Jo's cost (a mystery-shop quote should confirm this), that Mr Mattress copies the spec, and that VAT registration becomes compulsory at about 8 sets a month, which takes R2,226 off each set's profit unless it is priced in from day one.

## Mass chains stop near R21k; Dial-a-Bed flagships run to R130k

SA bed retail has two premium ladders, split at about **R20,000 a set**. The upper end of the mass-chain range is Sealy Crown Jewel, Serta, Restonic and Rest Assured, which sell queen and king sets at roughly R13k–R21k. Sleepmasters' Crown Jewel LaRochelle Firm queen set is **R18,499**, and its whole 33-product king-set range tops out at **R20,999** ([Sleepmasters](https://www.sleepmasters.co.za/sealy-crown-jewel-larochelle-firm-bed); [Sleepmasters King](https://www.sleepmasters.co.za/base-sets/size/king-beds?product_list_order=price&product_list_dir=desc)). Above that line, Dial-a-Bed carries 465 bed-set SKUs. They climb through Simmons Winchester, Healthsmart and Smart Response and Sealy Graphene Touch to a **R129,999** Simmons Beautyrest Black King XL ([Dial-a-Bed](https://www.dialabed.co.za/bed-sets?product_list_order=price&product_list_dir=desc)). Tempur asks **R46,950–R92,370 for the mattress alone** ([Vencasa](https://vencasa.co.za/product/tempur-pro-plus-mattress-2/)). Most manufacturers publish no prices, so retailer sale prices are the real market price, and those sales never end: nearly every premium Dial-a-Bed SKU shows a "was" price **15–20% higher** ([Dial-a-Bed p3](https://www.dialabed.co.za/bed-sets?product_list_order=price&product_list_dir=desc&p=3)).

Three features of this ladder matter for Big Jo's. First, **firmness earns no premium**. Graphene Touch Firm costs R10k less than the Luxury version of the same bed (R54,999 vs R64,999), so "firm" cannot justify a higher price ([Dial-a-Bed](https://www.dialabed.co.za/bed-sets?product_list_order=price&product_list_dir=desc)). Second, **length is cheap and capped**. Going from 188 to 200 cm adds a flat R1,000–R3,000 (Skyline Extra Firm King R18,999 vs King XL R20,999), Simmons charges nothing extra, no chain sells anything longer than 200 cm, and the standard widths are 152 and 183 cm ([Dial-a-Bed p4](https://www.dialabed.co.za/bed-sets?product_list_order=price&product_list_dir=desc&p=4); [Sleepmasters XL](https://www.sleepmasters.co.za/base-sets/extra-length-sizes?product_list_order=price&product_list_dir=desc)). Third, **chains do not sell on weight**. The only explicit rating found at a chain was Dunlopillo's Cooltouch Ultra Firm at **150 kg per side** ([Dial-a-Bed](https://www.dialabed.co.za/dunlopillo-cooltouch-ultra-firm-mattress-only)). A 160 × 210 cm bed rated 250 kg therefore has no chain equivalent at any price. Even so, a chain shopper who wants a "premium firm big bed" will expect to pay R19k–R31k for Sealy or Simmons, and Big Jo's has to make sense within that range.

## No SA rival sells past 200 cm or publishes 250 kg

The online field is thin, and its leader is based in Cape Town. **Sloom** (founded 2016, 22,000+ customers) sells a single adjustable foam mattress. The King XL is **R17,900**, a "Cape Town King" (214 cm wide × 200 cm long) is **R23,900**, and upholstered frames cost R9,000–R12,000, so a King XL with a frame comes to about **R28,400** ([Sloom products.json](https://sloom.co.za/products.json); [Sloom](https://sloom.co.za/)). Sloom has raised prices **81–88% since 2019**, when the King XL cost R9,900 ([Business Insider SA 2019](https://web.archive.org/web/20191208120110/https://www.businessinsider.co.za/we-compare-south-africas-mattresses-that-arrive-at-your-home-in-a-box-2019-7)). That climb shows SA DTC customers will pay up for a brand they trust. The other premium online brands, Meelu Hybrid (King XL R12,999) and SleepSport (King XL R23,599), publish **150 kg per person**, as Sloom does, and all of them stop at 200 cm ([Meelu](https://meelusleep.com/product/meelu-hybrid/); [SleepSport](https://www.sleepsport.co.za/wp-json/wc/store/v1/products?type=variation&per_page=100); [Sloom](https://sloom.co.za/products/sloom-original-mattress)).

Competition is sharpest in the heavy-duty niche, and **Mr Mattress** is the rival to watch. Its Ultra 200 is rated **200 kg per sleeper**. It is 30 cm tall, double-sided and built on a **"Chip 65" chip-foam core**, and it is made in the company's own Cape Town facility. The queen costs R9,199, the King XL base set **R14,699** on its permanent "sale" (R20,599 "regular"), and it comes with a 365-night trial, a 10-year non-pro-rata warranty and free delivery ([Mr Mattress products.json](https://www.mrmattress.co.za/products.json); [Mr Mattress heavy-sleeper guide](https://www.mrmattress.co.za/pages/best-mattress-for-heavy-sleepers-in-south-africa); [Mr Mattress Cape Town](https://www.mrmattress.co.za/pages/best-mattress-online-cape-town)). It owns the search content for heavy sleepers and names Sloom's 150 kg limit directly. It will also custom-cut any length, but **custom mattresses lose both the 365-night trial and the money-back guarantee** ([Mr Mattress XL](https://www.mrmattress.co.za/collections/extra-length-mattresses)).

Weight ratings in SA are unregulated marketing claims, and that includes ratings from Big Jo's own supplier. On its website, PBS says its Orthoflex range suits people "up to 100kg", while its Facebook page markets the same range for people "weighing up to 200kg". It rates its Executive at 120 kg and its Ortho-Delux at 200 kg ([PBS Orthoflex](https://pbsbeds.co.za/product/orthoflex-queen-bed-set/); [PBS Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/PeninsulaBedSalesCT/posts/-loved-by-customers-for-its-firm-supportive-comfort-the-orthoflex-range-is-built/1530391655767931/); [PBS Executive XL](https://pbsbeds.co.za/product/executive-single-mattress-only-extra-length/); [PBS Ortho-Delux](https://pbsbeds.co.za/product/ortho-delux-3-4-bed-set-extra-length/)). BlueCross's 150 Ultra is "150kg" on its retailer's product page and "220KG" on the same retailer's Facebook ([Brothers Beds](https://brothersbeds.com/product/blue-cross-150-ultra/); [Brothers Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/BrothersPineMattress/posts/%EF%B8%8F-built-for-bigger-built-for-better-sleep-bluecross-150-ultra-super-core-big-bod/1668723738588634/)). Budget sellers claim 200 kg on R2,399–R3,399 king mattresses ([Beds Plus](https://www.bedsplus.co.za/product/ortho-deluxe-foam-mattress-king-size/)). At 250 kg, Big Jo's would have the **highest published per-person rating found in SA**. But where numbers cost nothing to print, the number only becomes an asset once it is documented and tested.

The build does not set Big Jo's apart either. The chip-foam core is the same building block Mr Mattress uses in a R9,199 queen. And the **R7,930 single-unit quote sits inside PBS's own retail price range**: PBS sells its Chiropedic King XL mattress for R7,420 and its 200 kg Ortho-Delux ¾ XL set for R5,640 ([PBS](https://pbsbeds.co.za/product/executive-single-mattress-only-extra-length/); [PBS Ortho-Delux](https://pbsbeds.co.za/product/ortho-delux-3-4-bed-set-extra-length/)). That suggests, though only a mystery-shop quote can confirm it, that a customer who walks into PBS could buy something close to Big Jo's spec at roughly Big Jo's cost. PBS advertises that it cuts mattresses "to your exact measurements" in Montague Gardens, usually "within a few working days", with R450 delivery inside 55 km ([PBS Montague Gardens](https://pbsbeds.co.za/bed-shop-montague-gardens-mattresses-bed-sets-cape-town/)). Other Cape Town factories such as Sleepy Time in Parow and Dream Beds and Home sell custom and extra-length sets at roughly R3k–R10k ([Sleepy Time](https://sleepytimebedsfactory.com/); [Gumtree Western Cape](https://www.gumtree.co.za/s-western-cape/custom+mattress/v1l3100001q0p1)). Bariatric medical suppliers are not competitors. Their beds are narrow (Medop's 247 kg-rated bed has an 88 cm surface) and their products are pressure-care mattresses at R9k–R12k ([Medop](https://www.medop.co.za/product/bariatric-h-duty-electric-bed/); [Mr Mobility](https://mrmobility.co.za/product-category/beds/)).

The likely customer is broad more than very tall. SA men average **166.7 cm** ([Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/average-height-of-men?tab=table&country=~ZAF)), so men over 1.9 m are a sliver of the population; a rough estimate, not a measured figure, puts them well under 1%. Heavy men are a far larger pool. About **770,000 SA men had a BMI of 35 or more** in 2017, male obesity reached 15.3% by 2021 ([SAMJ 2025](https://samajournals.co.za/index.php/samj/article/download/3616/1555/23484)), and 44% of Western Cape men are overweight or obese, against 31% nationally ([SADHS 2016](https://dhsprogram.com/pubs/pdf/SR248/SR248.pdf)). The 210 cm length still matters to the many big men who are 1.85 m or taller, but the 250 kg rating reaches more buyers.

## US "built for big" beds fetch R21k–R35k, yet none goes long

The US has a mature plus-size category. Most of its models are reinforced pocket-coil hybrids, 29–39 cm thick, rated **450–550 lb (204–250 kg) per side**. At USD 1 = R16.65 ([findexify](https://findexify.com/forex/USD-ZAR/)), Titan Plus Elite sells at about **R21,200** on sale and Helix Plus at about **R21,300** ([Brooklyn Bedding](https://brooklynbedding.com/products/titan-elite); [Helix](https://helixsleep.com/products/helix-plus)). NapLab puts the category's average queen at about **R28,000** ([NapLab](https://naplab.com/mattress-reviews/titan-plus-elite-review/)). The only pure-play brand, **Big Fig**, sells its Classic queen at about **R28,300** on sale and its heavy-duty foundation at about R6,600, so a Big Fig set costs roughly **R34,900** ([Big Fig](https://www.bigfigmattress.com/products/big-fig-classic-mattress-queen?Size=Queen)). Saatva HD, the luxury ceiling, sells at about **R53,500** on sale ([Saatva](https://www.saatva.com/mattresses?sizes=queen)). These are US prices for US incomes, so they set an outer bound for Big Jo's rather than a target.

How much extra a heavy-duty version costs depends on brand positioning, not materials. Helix Plus costs **12.5%** more than Helix's standard Midnight, while Saatva HD costs **63–69%** more than the Saatva Classic ([NapLab](https://naplab.com/mattress-comparisons/helix-vs-helix-luxe-vs-helix-elite/); [Saatva](https://www.saatva.com/mattresses?sizes=queen)). Big Jo's at R24,999 would be **70% above** Mr Mattress's heavy-duty set, a gap the size of Saatva's. Saatva fills its gap with natural latex, an organic-cotton cover, white-glove delivery, free removal of the old mattress, a 365-night trial and a lifetime warranty ([Saatva HD](https://www.saatva.com/mattresses/saatva-hd)). That list is the template for what Big Jo's has to match.

Length is an open gap internationally too. Big Fig, Saatva HD, Titan and Helix all stop at California King (183 × 213 cm). Helix even markets its Plus as "Big & Tall" without making it any longer ([Helix](https://helixsleep.com/products/helix-plus); [Saatva HD](https://www.saatva.com/mattresses/saatva-hd)). In Europe, 160 × 210 cm is a recognised retail "special size" ([OTTO](https://www.otto.de/heimtextilien/matratzen/?sondergroesse=160x210-cm)), and US custom extra-long specialists charge USD 2,500–3,500 (≈R42k–R58k) for "giant" beds ([Mattress Insider](https://www.mattressinsider.com/extra-long-mattress.html)). Nobody abroad combines "big" and "tall" in one product.

The US leaders follow the same playbook. They put kg or lb ratings in the headline, use confident and inclusive copy ("built to support big lives"), and back their claims with outside proof: certifications, a chiropractic association endorsement and lab reviews. They tell founder stories and, increasingly, use athletes. Big Fig signed NFL star Myles Garrett and is broadening beyond plus-size customers because weight-loss drugs are shrinking that market ([Cleveland Magazine](https://clevelandmagazine.com/articles/myles-garrett-highlights-big-fig-s-expansion-beyond-plus-size-market/)). Height does not shrink on GLP-1 drugs, which makes "big and tall men" a more durable position than "plus-size". Two cautions follow. NapLab measured WinkBed Plus foam densities **below the manufacturer's stated figures**, so published specs get checked ([NapLab](https://naplab.com/mattress-reviews/winkbed-plus-review/)). And Big Jo's 25 cm profile is thinner than every US plus-size bed, where NapLab's average is 12.1 in, about 31 cm ([NapLab](https://naplab.com/mattress-reviews/titan-plus-elite-review/)); in this category, thickness reads as "heavy-duty". The 20 mm latex layer helps, since Saatva sells its 1-inch latex layer on durability, buoyancy and breathability ([Saatva HD](https://www.saatva.com/mattresses/saatva-hd)). It only counts as premium, though, if Big Jo's can say whether the latex is natural or certified.

## Fee-laden trials and missing services leave the promise wide open

SA chain trials are exchanges with conditions attached, not refunds. Sleepmasters' 120-night guarantee requires a Sleepmasters protector on the same order, a 30-night minimum and a single exchange, and charges a **10% admin fee plus redelivery** ([Sleepmasters](https://www.sleepmasters.co.za/sealy-crown-jewel-larochelle-firm-bed)). Dial-a-Bed's 100-night exchange gives no refunds, charges **R395 for steam-cleaning** plus a second delivery fee, and **excludes custom sizes** and its flagship ranges ([Dial-a-Bed](https://www.dialabed.co.za/100-night-comfort-exchange)). The online brands offer refunds. Sloom refunds between days 60 and 100 and reports a return rate under 3% ([Sloom](https://sloom.co.za/); [Bizcommunity](https://www.bizcommunity.com/article/sloom-founder-shares-10-lessons-from-building-south-africas-mattress-disruptor-967404a)), yet its Hellopeter reviews complain about return fees and replies that average **119 hours** ([Hellopeter](https://www.hellopeter.com/sloom)). Mr Mattress's 365 nights do not apply to custom sizes. As a result, **no SA seller offers a trial on a custom-size bed**.

Warranties follow a similar pattern. The SA norm is a 2–3-year guarantee plus an 8–15-year "service" warranty: 3 + 15 years on Crown Jewel at The Mattress Warehouse and 2 + 8 on Dunlopillo ([The Mattress Warehouse](https://www.themattresswarehouse.co.za/product/sealy-crown-jewel-lindsay-plush-mattress/); [Dial-a-Bed](https://www.dialabed.co.za/dunlopillo-cooltouch-ultra-firm-mattress-only)). Sloom pairs a 2-year fault guarantee with a "25-year service warranty", and Mr Mattress offers 10 years not pro-rated ([Sloom](https://sloom.co.za/products/sloom-original-mattress); [Mr Mattress](https://www.mrmattress.co.za/pages/best-mattress-for-heavy-sleepers-in-south-africa)). The one weight-linked guarantee found is from the UK's Big Bed Company: **five years at up to 230 kg per sleeper** ([The Big Bed Company](https://thebigbedcompany.com/strong-reinforced-beds-for-heavy-people)). That is a credible model for "guaranteed at 250 kg".

Big Jo's is ahead on speed and can be ahead on service. Sleepmasters charges R295–R695 for delivery and takes 6–8 weeks on special orders. Dial-a-Bed charges in-store orders R549–R1,200 by zone and takes up to 21 days for custom orders, and its flagships ship in 7–10 working days ([Sleepmasters](https://www.sleepmasters.co.za/sealy-crown-jewel-larochelle-firm-bed); [Dial-a-Bed delivery](https://www.dialabed.co.za/delivery-information)). PBS's **3–5 working days for a custom build** beats every custom lead time found. None of the chain pages checked mentions removing the old bed, whereas Saatva does it free with white-glove setup. Shoppers also expect to see an instalment figure on the page. Sleepmasters shows its R18,499 set as R901 a month and offers PayJustNow, Payflex and Mobicred, and Sloom offers Float over six months ([Sleepmasters](https://www.sleepmasters.co.za/sealy-crown-jewel-larochelle-firm-bed); [Sloom](https://sloom.co.za/products/sloom-original-mattress)).

Bedding is the hidden opportunity. SA extra-length sheets are 200 cm long and cost about R270–R310 ([Edura](https://eduraonline.co.za/products/180-thread-count-poly-percale-fitted-sheets-extra-length-extra-depth); [Takealot](https://www.takealot.com/george-mason-200tc-cotton-extra-length-depth-fitted-sheet-grey/PLID96932195)). A 160 × 210 cm mattress fits neither queen nor king sheets, and the custom makers (Falucca's Cape Town studio, Bedworld, Linen and Co) work on quotes only ([Falucca](https://www.faluccalinen.co.za/2019/09/11/unusual-bed-size-custom-bedding/); [Bedworld](https://bedworld.co.za/custom-size-sheets/); [Linen and Co](https://www.linenco.co.za/pages/custom-sizing)). Putting one custom cotton fitted sheet and a protector in the box solves a problem that buying the bed creates, and every replacement sheet becomes repeat revenue.

Together, these norms point to a promise that small competitors cannot easily match and large ones will not. Big Jo's should offer free white-glove delivery, setup and old-bed removal across Cape Town. It should give a 100-night comfort guarantee with no protector purchase and no admin fee, which first offers a free firmness or layer adjustment and then a refund less a published collection fee. It should back the bed with a **10-year guarantee at 250 kg per person** and a stated sag-depth limit, valid only on the Big Jo's base, just as US brands tie their warranties to their own foundations. And it should include custom bedding in the box and take PayJustNow, Payflex, Mobicred and Float. The trial is affordable: at Sloom's 3% return rate, writing off every returned custom set (R7,930 plus R900 of two-way logistics) costs about **R265 per set sold**.

## R24,999 a set leaves R17,069 to pay for proof

The recommended prices are **R24,999 for the set**, **R19,999 for the mattress alone** and **R5,999 for the base alone**, so the set saves R999. That bundle saving gives buyers a reason to take the set, as Sloom's bundles do, without inventing a "was" price. Because 160 × 210 cm is non-standard, no customer already owns a matching base or frame. The set is therefore the real product, and the mattress-only price mainly serves replacements. The mattress alone earns R14,019 (70.1%) and the base alone R4,049 (67.5%). Show the set as "or 3 × R8,333 interest-free".

Pricing off the heavy-duty specialists would put Big Jo's at R14k–R18k, roughly 1.8–2.3 times cost. At R17,999, though, Big Jo's becomes a slightly dearer Mr Mattress with no reviews, and **R9,619 a set** would have to cover advertising, the load test, the trial reserve, bedding and payment fees. The SA brands that survived raised their prices, while low-price entrants faded: Genie's store is entirely out of stock and only two of the five bed-in-a-box brands compared in 2019 still clearly trade online ([Genie](https://geniebeds.co.za/wp-json/wc/store/v1/products?per_page=50); [Business Insider SA 2019](https://web.archive.org/web/20191208120110/https://www.businessinsider.co.za/we-compare-south-africas-mattresses-that-arrive-at-your-home-in-a-box-2019-7)). At R29,999 or more, Big Jo's would compete with Simmons Winchester Firm (R30,799, with showrooms) and approach a Big Fig set (≈R34,900), which has 15-gauge coils, 8,000+ claimed customers and a chiropractic endorsement ([Big Fig](https://www.bigfigmattress.com/products/big-fig-classic-mattress-queen?Size=Queen); [Big Fig blog](https://www.bigfigmattress.com/blog/saatva-hd-vs-big-fig-mattress)), while offering a chip core, a 25 cm profile and no reviews. R24,999 sits just above the Sleepmasters ceiling, level with Skyline and Sova king sets, and 12% below Sloom's King XL with a frame. Once the independent test and about 50 genuine reviews are in, Big Jo's should **step up to R27,999**.

VAT changes the rand figures but not the percentages. The compulsory registration threshold rose to **R2.3 million** of annual turnover on 1 April 2026 ([SARS](https://www.sars.gov.za/faq/what-is-the-new-threshold-for-vat-registration/)). At R24,999, Big Jo's crosses it at about **7.7 sets a month**, and the 10–20-set target means R3.0m–R6.0m a year, so registration is a near-certainty in year one. Once registered, Big Jo's can claim back the VAT on PBS's invoices, so the 68.3% margin holds, but profit per set falls by **R2,226 to R14,843**. Treat R24,999 as a VAT-inclusive price from launch so customers never see a 15% jump. At 10–20 sets a month, the pre-VAT contribution is **R166k–R332k a month**, before marketing, bedding, payment fees and overheads.

### What the price has to be backed by

**Fit** is the only claim no SA competitor can make, so Big Jo's should sell "the only 210 cm bed in South Africa" as a complete system of mattress, matched base and custom sheets. **The rating** has to be earned on paper and in a lab. First, PBS should put the 250 kg rating and its test basis in writing, given the conflicting figures on its own channels. Then Big Jo's should commission an independent rolling-load durability test at 250 kg, since Mr Mattress already cites an EN 1957 test at 140 kg ([Mr Mattress](https://www.mrmattress.co.za/pages/best-mattress-for-heavy-person-south-africa)), and publish the foam densities and the latex type, because specs get checked. **Service** is the cheapest premium to deliver: the 3–5-day build, white-glove delivery with removal of the old bed, a fee-free trial and replies within hours rather than Sloom's 119.

**The brand** should take the confident "built for big lives" tone and point it at stature and sport (tall men, rugby frames, a founder who never fit a bed), and avoid the clinical "bariatric" language of the UK specialists. Finally, **the 25 cm profile** has to be explained (density over height) or fixed with a topper option. Mr Mattress is 30 cm, Dunlopillo's Cooltouch 35 cm and Crown Jewel Lindsay 39.5 cm, and big buyers read thickness as strength ([Mr Mattress](https://www.mrmattress.co.za/products/ultra-200-memory-foam-mattress); [Dial-a-Bed](https://www.dialabed.co.za/dunlopillo-cooltouch-ultra-firm-mattress-only); [The Mattress Warehouse](https://www.themattresswarehouse.co.za/product/sealy-crown-jewel-lindsay-plush-mattress/)).

### Risks, and the phone calls that close the gaps

**Replication is the biggest risk.** Big Jo's single-unit price sits inside PBS's own retail range, and PBS sells custom beds direct to the public, so Big Jo's needs volume pricing that is a real trade discount, a branded or exclusive spec, and supplier-backed warranty terms. Relying on one supplier makes this worse. **Mr Mattress could respond**: it custom-cuts any size, owns the heavy-sleeper search traffic and could publish a 250 kg rating, although its custom beds currently carry no trial, which is Big Jo's opening. **The 160 cm width** gives two large sleepers 80 cm each, narrower than a 183 cm King XL, which makes a 183 × 210 cm version the obvious second size. **Shoppers are trained on permanent sales**, so a single honest price will look expensive next to "was" prices unless the bundle and the promise are explained. And **growth is slow and costly**: Sloom took three years to make money ([SA Decor & Design](https://www.sadecor.co.za/interior-design-blog/bedrooms/furniture-mattresses/years-of-rejection-rockets-sloom-into-a-sleeping-success/)).

Several gaps in the market picture are best closed by phone or in person:

## Conclusion

Big Jo's is not really competing on mattresses. It is selling a solved problem: a big man who has never fit a bed gets a bed, base and sheets that fit, with a guarantee written at his weight, delivered within about a week. No South African seller offers that package, and no international heavy-duty leader makes the bed longer. What limits the price is not the foam. It is the do-it-yourself route, which on today's evidence costs roughly R8.4k from the same factory (unconfirmed until a mystery-shop quote), plus whatever proof and service Big Jo's adds on top. Every rand above Mr Mattress therefore has to correspond to something a customer can see, test or claim.

Treat R24,999 as a launch price, not a fixed one. Sloom's 81–88% climb shows SA buyers will follow a DTC brand upmarket once it has proved itself, and Big Fig's turn toward athletes points the same way: a brand built on stature lasts longer than one built on "plus-size". The order of work matters more than the exact figure: lock in supplier terms and a documented rating first, then test, then launch at R24,999 and raise the price as reviews arrive. Launching on price before the proof exists would put the brand's premium in the hands of its supplier.
